*Paulos’s letter to*
# Filemon

## Introduction

Filemon was a follower of Yeshua and part of a group of believers in the city of Kolossai. He owned a slave named Onesimos, who ran away and eventually met Paulos the missionary in prison in Romai. There, thanks to Paulos sharing the good message, Onesimos became a believer in Yeshua. In *Paulos’s letter to Filemon*, Paulos asks Filemon to repair the relationship with Onesimos: he’s sending Onesimos back, and he wants Filemon to take him in, not just because he’s forgiven him, but because Onesimos is now a fellow believer and therefore a brother.

This is the shortest of Paulos’s surviving letters, and it’s a masterclass in asking for something difficult without simply giving an order. Notice how often he chooses to ask instead of command. Footnotes in this edition are labelled *Decoder* (for slang) and *Nerd note* (for background).

**Main components of Paulos’s letter**

- Introduction (1-3)
- Praising Filemon (4-7)
- Paulos’s request about Onesimos (8-22)
- Ending (23-25)

## Chapter 1

<sup>**1**</sup> This letter is from Paulos, a prisoner of messiah Yeshua (Jesus; Grk. Yesus), and our brother Timotheos, to our dear co-worker Filemon (Philemon),
<sup>**2**</sup> and to our sister Apfia and our fellow soldier Arkippos, *(cf. Col 4:17)* and to the assembly that meets in your home.[^1]
<sup>**3**</sup> Grace and peace to you all from our father God and from the master Yeshua the messiah.
### Paulos’s prayer and gratefulness

<sup>**4**</sup> Filemon (Philemon), I’m always thanking my God and mentioning you in my prayers,
<sup>**5**</sup> because I keep hearing about your faith in the master Yeshua (Jesus; Grk. Yesus) and your love for all the believers.
<sup>**6**</sup> I’m praying that the way you share your faith will become active as you recognize every good thing we have in the messiah.[^2]
<sup>**7**</sup> I was so happy and encouraged by your love, because the hearts of the believers have been refreshed by you, brother.
### Paulos’s request about Onesimos

<sup>**8**</sup> Because I’m united with the messiah, I could be bold and simply tell you what you need to do,
<sup>**9**</sup> but because of love I’d rather appeal to you instead, as Paulos, an unc by now, and also a prisoner for Yeshua (Jesus; Grk. Yesus) the messiah.[^3]
<sup>**10**</sup> I’m appealing to you about Onesimos, *(cf. Col 4:9)* who became my spiritual ‘son’ while I’ve been in prison.[^4]
<sup>**11**</sup> Before, he was useless to you, but now he’s useful to both of us.[^5]

<sup>**12**</sup> I’m sending him back to you (even though he’s my own heart,
<sup>**13**</sup> and I wanted to keep him here) so that he could serve me here while I’m in chains for the good message,
<sup>**14**</sup> but I didn’t want to do anything without hearing what you think, so that the good you do isn’t forced, but comes from you choosing it.

<sup>**15**</sup> Maybe this is why he was separated from you for a while: so that you could have him back for good,
<sup>**16**</sup> no longer as a slave, but as better than a slave, a dear brother. He’s that to me most of all, but he should be even more so to you, both as a person and in Yahweh.[^6]

<sup>**17**</sup> So if you consider me a partner, welcome him the way you’d welcome me.
<sup>**18**</sup> And if he wronged you or owes you anything, put it on my tab.
<sup>**19**</sup> I, Paulos, am writing this part in my own handwriting: I’ll pay back whatever he owes you, and I won’t even bring up that you basically owe me your whole self.[^7]
<sup>**20**</sup> Yes, brother, since we’re both in Yahweh, let me get some benefit from you: refresh my heart in the messiah.

<sup>**21**</sup> I’m writing to you because I’m sure you’ll do what I’m asking; in fact, I know you’ll do even more than I ask.
### Paulos’s final greetings

<sup>**22**</sup> Also, get a place ready for me to stay, because I’m hoping to visit, thanks to all of you praying for me.
<sup>**23**</sup> Epafras (Epaphras), *(cf. Col 1:7; 4:12)* my fellow prisoner in Yeshua (Jesus; Grk. Yesus) the messiah, sends his greetings,
<sup>**24**</sup> and so do my fellow workers Markos, *(cf. Acts 12:12,25; 13:13; 15:37-39; Col 4:10)* Aristarkos, *(cf. Acts 19:29; 27:2; Col 4:10)* Demas, *(cf. Col 4:14; 2Tim 4:10)* and Lukas (Luke). *(cf. Col 4:14; 2Tim 4:11)*

<sup>**25**</sup> May the grace of our master Yeshua the messiah be with the spirit of you all. May it be so.

[^1]: **Nerd note.** The letter is addressed to the whole house assembly, not just Filemon. Verses 3 and 25 use ‘you all’, so the request about Onesimos is made with an audience listening.
[^2]: **Nerd note.** This verse is hard to translate even from the Greek. The OET draft this edition is based on flagged it as unclear, and translators disagree about it, so treat this as one reasonable reading, not the final word.
[^3]: **Decoder.** ‘Unc’ is slang, short for ‘uncle’, for an older guy who’s a bit out of touch, though sometimes it’s said with respect. Paulos is calling himself an older man, and the Greek word here does mean that.
[^4]: **Nerd note.** ‘Onesimos’ was a real Greek name with the sense ‘useful’, which sets up the wordplay in the next verse.
[^5]: **Nerd note.** The Greek pair here is achrēstos (‘useless’) and euchrēstos (‘useful’), a pun that echoes his name.
[^6]: **Nerd note.** Scholars still debate whether Paulos is asking Filemon to free Onesimos or simply to treat him differently. What’s clear is the reframing: ‘brother’ where Roman law said ‘property’.
[^7]: **Nerd note.** Rhetoricians call this move paralipsis: mentioning something by announcing that you won’t mention it. Paulos is very polite and very good at this.
