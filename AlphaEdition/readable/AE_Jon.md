# Yonah (Jonah)

## Introduction

Okay, so here’s the setup. A prophet named Yonah (you probably know him as ‘Jonah’) lived in northern Yisrael/Israel around 250 years after David became king, i.e., in the eighth century BC, perhaps around 785 BC. God gave him a simple assignment: go to the city of Nineveh in Ashur/Assyria and speak out against it. Yonah’s response? He bought a ticket in the opposite direction. That makes this account unusual among the prophetic books, because its main character is a prophet who *didn’t* do what God told him. He avoided the assignment because he suspected God would be merciful and call off the punishment if the people of that city turned away from their sins. Then came a storm, a huge fish, a beach, and a second chance, and Yonah ended up in Nineveh after all. And it worked: the king led a movement of turning back towards God. Yonah was furious that God then didn’t destroy the city. Yes, really.

The author also wants us to notice that Yahweh is in control of everything he made: the ocean, the wind, a fish, a plant, a grub. But most of all, we’re told how loving and merciful God is, because even the enemies of his chosen people were forgiven and saved after they turned away from their sins. (Notice who’s still arguing at the very end of the story. It’s the prophet.)

A note on how this one is told: the narration and the crowd scenes are in the way people actually tell a story to their friends, with slang, in the voice you’d hear at lunch. Anything that might need translating gets a footnote labelled *Decoder*, and anything that needs scholarly background gets a footnote labelled *Nerd note*. Tap any highlighted bit to see the plain wording. God’s words, the prayers, and the darkest moments stay plain on purpose; some things aren’t for joking. Slang ages fast, so if a word here already sounds embarrassing by the time you read it, that’s not a mistake. That’s how slang works.

**The story in four parts**

- Yonah runs from Yahweh’s assignment (1:1-17)
- Yonah’s prayer from inside the fish (2:1-10)
- Yonah gets a second chance and goes to Nineveh (3:1-10)
- Yonah has a meltdown over God’s mercy (4:1-11)

## Chapter 1

### Yonah gets an assignment and flees the country

*Northern kingdom*

<sup>**1**</sup> Okay, lore drop: Yahweh gave Yonah (Jonah), Amitay’s son, an assignment. *(cf. 2Ki 14:25)*[^1][^2]
<sup>**2**</sup> “Go to Nineveh, that huge city, and call it out. I’ve seen how evil they are.”

<sup>**3**</sup> Yonah’s response: absolutely not, bro. Zero percent. He ghosted Yahweh, no cap, so he went down to Yafo (Joppa), found a ship about to leave for Tarshish, paid the fare, and hopped on, headed for Tarshish to get away from Yahweh. That plan was so delulu it hurt, but he committed. Main character behavior.[^3][^4][^5][^6][^7]

<sup>**4**</sup> Then Yahweh sent a massive wind over the sea, and the storm got so unhinged that everyone on board was convinced the ship was straight-up cooked.[^8]
<sup>**5**</sup> The sailors were tweaking. Every single one of them was screaming to their own gods for help, and they yeeted the cargo overboard to make the ship lighter. And Yonah? Down in the hold, horizontal, out cold: major aura, or major denial.[^9][^10][^11]

<sup>**6**</sup> The ship’s captain pulled up on him and was like, “Bro?? You’re SLEEPING?? Right now, fr? Get UP and pray to your god. Maybe he’ll notice us and we won’t die.”[^12][^13]

<sup>**7**</sup> Then the sailors went, “Let’s draw straws and find out whose fault this disaster is.” So they drew straws, and Yonah got picked. Shocker. Literally nobody is surprised.
<sup>**8**</sup> So they hit him with the full interrogation: “Okay, explain. Why is this disaster happening to us? What’s your job, bro? Where are you from? What country? What’s your nationality, fr?”[^14]

<sup>**9**</sup> And he said to them, “I’m an Ivri (Hebrew), and I fear Yahweh, the god of heaven, who made the sea and the land.”[^15]

<sup>**10**</sup> The men were deadass freaking out. “Bruh. WHAT did you DO?!” they asked, because Yonah had already told them that he was running from Yahweh.[^16][^17]

<sup>**11**</sup> Then they asked him, “Okay, so what do we do with you to make the sea chill?” Because the storm was getting worse by the second.

<sup>**12**</sup> And he said to them, “Pick me up and throw me into the sea, and it will calm down. I know this terrible storm came because of me.”

<sup>**13**</sup> Even so, the men really tried, bless them, to row back to land, but they couldn’t. The sea was not having it and just kept getting wilder and wilder.[^18]
<sup>**14**</sup> So they called out to Yahweh: “We’re begging you, Yahweh: please don’t let us die because of this man, and don’t hold us guilty for this. You’re the one who made it happen.”
<sup>**15**</sup> Then they picked Yonah up and threw him into the sea, and the sea straight-up stopped.
<sup>**16**</sup> The men were so in awe of Yahweh, and lowkey terrified of him, that they offered a sacrifice to him and made vows.[^19]

<sup>**17**</sup> Then Yahweh ordered a huge fish to swallow Yonah (Jonah), and he stayed in the belly of the fish for three days and three nights. Three. Whole. Days. In. A. Fish. *(cf. Mat 12:40)*[^20]

[^1]: **Decoder.** ‘Lore drop’ means a chunk of background story, usually delivered all at once.
[^2]: **Nerd note.** A prophet named Yonah, son of Amitay, is also mentioned in 2 Kings 14:25, which is the basis for placing him in the 700s BC.
[^3]: **Decoder.** ‘Ghosted’ means cutting someone off and vanishing without a word. It’s usually said about texts and dating, and here it’s about God.
[^4]: **Decoder.** ‘No cap’ means ‘for real’, with no lie.
[^5]: **Decoder.** ‘Delulu’ is short for ‘delusional’, and it means hopelessly unrealistic. Trying to outrun God by boat is a strong candidate.
[^6]: **Decoder.** ‘Main character’ describes someone who acts like the whole story is about them. In this story, he’s not wrong that it’s about him, just not in the way he thinks.
[^7]: **Nerd note.** Tarshish is thought to be far to the west across the Mediterranean (Spain is a popular guess, but scholars aren’t certain). Nineveh was to the east. Yonah didn’t just leave; he picked the opposite direction.
[^8]: **Decoder.** ‘Cooked’ means finished, done for.
[^9]: **Decoder.** ‘Tweaking’ means acting wildly, out of control. Professional sailors, in a storm, would know.
[^10]: **Decoder.** ‘Yeeted’ means threw hard and fast, with no care where it landed.
[^11]: **Decoder.** ‘Aura’ is slang for effortless coolness. Sleeping through a storm that has professional sailors praying earns Yonah either a lot of aura or none, depending on whom you ask.
[^12]: **Decoder.** ‘Pulled up on’ means showed up in person, usually to confront someone.
[^13]: **Decoder.** ‘Fr’ is short for ‘for real’.
[^14]: **Decoder.** ‘Fr’ is short for ‘for real’.
[^15]: **Nerd note.** The Hebrew root for ‘fear’ shows up in verses 5, 9, 10 and 16, sometimes as ‘revere’ (here) and sometimes as ‘terrified’ (the sailors). Also notice the irony: Yonah says Yahweh made the sea and the land while sailing away across the sea.
[^16]: **Decoder.** ‘Deadass’ means seriously, no joke.
[^17]: **Decoder.** ‘Bruh’ is a reaction word, roughly ‘come on, really?’
[^18]: **Decoder.** ‘Not having it’ means refusing to put up with something. Here, the sea refuses to be rowed on.
[^19]: **Decoder.** ‘Lowkey’ means somewhat or quietly. It’s the opposite of ‘highkey’.
[^20]: **Nerd note.** The Hebrew says ‘huge fish’, not ‘whale’. The whale tradition comes from later translations, where the Greek used a word that means something like ‘sea monster’.

## Chapter 2

### Yonah’s prayer from inside the fish

<sup>**1**</sup> Then Yonah (Jonah) prayed to Yahweh his god from inside the fish. Yes, inside.
<sup>**2**</sup> And he said,

> “I called out to you, Yahweh, when I was in trouble, and you answered me. From the valley of death I cried for help, and you heard my voice.
> <sup>**3**</sup> You threw me right into the deep, into the heart of the sea, and the flood surrounds me. All your waves are crashing over me.

> <sup>**4**</sup> I thought, ‘I’ve been driven out of your sight, but I’ll look again towards your holy temple.’

> <sup>**5**</sup> The water came up to my neck; the deep was all around me. Reeds were wrapped around my head.

> <sup>**6**</sup> I went down to the roots of the mountains, locked out of ever returning to land. But you saved my life from the grave, Yahweh my god.

> <sup>**7**</sup> When my life was draining away, I remembered you, Yahweh, and my prayer reached you in your holy temple.

> <sup>**8**</sup> People who chase lifeless gods abandon their loyalty to you.

> <sup>**9**</sup> But I’ll thank you out loud and offer you a sacrifice. I made a vow and I’ll keep it, because Yahweh is my rescuer.”[^21]

<sup>**10**</sup> Then Yahweh spoke to the fish, and it yeeted Yonah (Jonah) onto dry land: respawn complete.[^22][^23]

[^21]: **Nerd note.** Many readers have noticed that this prayer is all thanksgiving. There is no ‘sorry’ anywhere in it.
[^22]: **Decoder.** ‘Yeeted’ means threw hard and fast, with no care for the landing.
[^23]: **Decoder.** ‘Respawn’ is a video-game word for coming back after you’ve been defeated. Gamers would call this one a respawn, though the beach is not a standard spawn point.

## Chapter 3

### Second chance, same assignment

<sup>**1**</sup> And Yahweh spoke to Yonah (Jonah) round two:
<sup>**2**</sup> “Go to Nineveh, that huge city, and announce the message I’m giving you.”

<sup>**3**</sup> So this time Yonah finally understood the assignment and skipped the side quests and went straight to Nineveh, locked in. It was an enormous city; like, it took three days to get across.[^24][^25][^26]
<sup>**4**</sup> Yonah went one day’s travel into the city and announced, no cap, that Nineveh was cooked: “Nineveh is going to be destroyed in forty days!” That was the whole pitch. *(cf. Mat 12:41; Luk 11:32)*[^27][^28][^29]

<sup>**5**</sup> The people of Nineveh believed God, and they speedran the whole turnaround: they announced a time of fasting, and from the least to the most important, literally everyone put on sackcloth as a sign of sorrow for what they’d done.[^30]

<sup>**6**</sup> When the news reached the king of Nineveh, he got down from his throne, took off his robe, put on sackcloth, and sat in a pile of ashes. The KING. Himself. No cap.[^31]
<sup>**7**</sup> Then he had a statement written out and blasted all over Nineveh: “Royal decree, no exceptions, fr: no people or animals, whether herds or flocks, may graze or eat anything, and they can’t even drink water.[^32]
<sup>**8**</sup> Everybody, animals included, wears sackcloth, and everyone goes all in: cry out to God and turn away from the evil and violent things you’ve been doing.
<sup>**9**</sup> Maybe God will pull a U-turn, change his mind, and drop his fierce anger, and maybe we won’t die.”

<sup>**10**</sup> God saw what they did, how they turned away from their evil ways, and he changed his mind about the destruction he’d said he would send on them. He didn’t go through with it.

[^24]: **Decoder.** ‘Understood the assignment’ means did exactly what was asked, and did it well. Compare Yonah’s first attempt.
[^25]: **Decoder.** ‘Side quests’ are optional detours in a video game, away from the main mission. Yonah’s trip to Tarshish was one, and a big one.
[^26]: **Decoder.** ‘Locked in’ means fully focused. This time, Yonah is.
[^27]: **Decoder.** ‘No cap’ means ‘for real’, with no lie.
[^28]: **Decoder.** ‘Cooked’ means finished, done for.
[^29]: **Nerd note.** In Hebrew, Yonah’s whole sermon is five words. The last word can mean ‘overthrown’, and Nineveh’s next move is to turn itself around.
[^30]: **Decoder.** ‘Speedran’ comes from gaming, where a speedrun means finishing as fast as possible. Nineveh’s turnaround took one sermon.
[^31]: **Nerd note.** Notice the order: the people respond in verse 5, and only then does the news reach the king in verse 6. In this story, the change starts at the bottom.
[^32]: **Decoder.** ‘Fr’ is short for ‘for real’.

## Chapter 4

### Yonah has a meltdown

<sup>**1**</sup> But Yonah (Jonah) was full-on crashing out and big mad about the whole situation.[^33]
<sup>**2**</sup> And he said to Yahweh, “Okay, Yahweh, isn’t this exactly what I said would happen when I was still in my own country? That’s why I ran away to Tarshish the first time: I knew that you’re a caring, forgiving god, slow to get angry, full of kindness, and not wanting to cause suffering. *(cf. Exo 34:6)*[^34]
<sup>**3**</sup> So now, Yahweh, please kill me, because I’d rather die than live like this.” *(cf. 1Ki 19:4)*

<sup>**4**</sup> But Yahweh said, “Do you really think it’s right to be this angry?”

<sup>**5**</sup> And Yonah went outside the city to the east, built himself a little fort, and sat in its shade with a front-row seat to see what would happen to the city.
<sup>**6**</sup> Then Yahweh God commanded a plant to grow up over Yonah, to shade his head and ease his misery. Yonah was highkey obsessed with the plant. Bestie status: plant.[^35][^36]
<sup>**7**</sup> Then at dawn the next day God commanded a grub, and it attacked the plant, which got absolutely cooked. Oof.
<sup>**8**</sup> And when the sun came up, God sent a brutal east wind and the sun beat down on Yonah’s head. He felt faint and asked for his life to end, saying, “It’s better for me to die than to live.”

<sup>**9**</sup> And God said to Yonah, “Do you really think it’s right to be angry about the plant?”

And Yonah replied, “Yes, I’m angry enough to die!”

<sup>**10**</sup> Then Yahweh said, “You felt sorry for a plant that you didn’t plant or work for. It shot up in a day and died in a day,
<sup>**11**</sup> so shouldn’t I feel sorry for the huge city of Nineveh, which has so many people in it who don’t know right from wrong, over 120,000 people, not to mention all the livestock!”[^37]

[^33]: **Decoder.** ‘Crashing out’ is current slang for a full emotional meltdown.
[^34]: **Nerd note.** This echoes a famous description of God in Exodus 34:6. Most people treat that description as good news. Yonah treats it as a spoiler.
[^35]: **Decoder.** ‘Highkey’ means openly and fully, the opposite of ‘lowkey’.
[^36]: **Nerd note.** This is the only place in the whole story where Yonah is described as happy, and it’s about a plant.
[^37]: **Nerd note.** The book ends on a question, and Yonah never answers it on the page. Whether he comes around is left to the reader.
